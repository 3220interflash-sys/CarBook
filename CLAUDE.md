# CarBook

A vehicle ownership companion for Sri Lanka. Owners never miss a deadline, keep every service record, and know what to ask their mechanic. The owner is not a developer, so explain technical choices in plain language and keep changes small.

## Run it
Open a terminal in this folder and run `python3 -m http.server 8080`, then visit http://localhost:8080. No build step. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

## Files
- `index.html`: page shell
- `styles.css`: all styling (design tokens at the top)
- `strings.js`: every word the app shows, in English, Sinhala and Tamil. Missing translations fall back to English
- `app.js`: data, rules, forms and views. Text comes from `strings.js` through `t('key')`, never typed straight into the code
- `website/`: the public website, now branded MotorPal with the Four pillars logo (one page, `website/index.html`, with app screenshots in `website/img/`). Same Glow look as the app (November 2026): near-black green page with a faint mint grid, dark panels outlined in mint, icons in glowing outlined circles, mint buttons with a glow (the "Glow finish" block at the end of its styles; the black car has a mint rim light so it shows on the dark page). Plus Jakarta Sans. Under the menu bar runs a neon mint pulse line (`.pulse`, moved by a small script at the end of the page): it blips like a heart monitor, traces a car's side profile in the middle with the wheels lighting up, holds it for a moment, fades, and repeats (November 2026; it replaced a driving black car, and before that a flat cartoon car the owner found too game-like). The owner tried a formal airline-style version and preferred this one. It links to the app with `../`. The app's offline helper ignores this folder. When the app's look changes, retake the screenshots
- `sw.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `icon.svg`, `icon-*.png`: make it installable and work offline. After changing any app file, bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` so phones pick up the new copy. A new file must also be added to `FILES` there

## Product rules
- **Car health (the heart of the app, November 2026):** Home opens with Car health: a tile for every part that wears out (engine oil, battery, brake pads, tyres, wheel alignment, spark plugs, engine tune-up, air filter, AC filter, brake fluid, coolant, gearbox oil, wiper blades), each with a life bar and how long is left, plus a ring for how many parts are on track. Life comes from the last record and the default intervals in `INTERVALS` (`partLife` in `app.js`); parts that do not apply to the vehicle are hidden (`partFits`). Fuel is a smaller feature: it lives in the Money tab, not on Home. The fill-up form asks for a photo of the dashboard first, because the odometer reading keeps Car health up to date (reading it automatically needs the photo-reading backend).
- **Parts catalogue (November 2026):** about 230 parts from the owner's maintenance checklist research (`research/maintenance-checklist/`), built into `catalogue.js` and `strings.js` (`cat_<id>_name`, `_check`, `_typical`) by `development/tools/build-catalogue.py`. On the Health tab owners type to search or browse by group, then add a part: they see how often to check it and its typical replacement, get a suggested reminder (the shorter end of the typical range, editable), can enter when it was last done, and can set their own reminder date. Condition-based parts have no suggested interval, only the owner's date. Picks are stored in `db.tracked` (`comp` is `c:<id>` or a built-in part); service records for catalogue parts use kind `c:<id>`. Any part, built-in or not, can have a reminder date.
- **Your records:** one place for warranties, service records and part changes (`warrantyList`, `serviceList`, `PART_CHANGES`).
- **Phase 1 (now):** deadlines and renewals, maintenance status with plain explanations, a year-ahead plan with estimated service dates, fuel and spending trends (1 month to 5 years, compared with the period before), service and fuel records, and Ask (a chat about the owner's records, plus questions for the mechanic).
- **Fuel (November 2026):** a fill-up records amount spent, litres and the odometer; distance is the odometer gap since the previous fill-up, and km per litre is that distance divided by litres (`fuelRows`, `fuelStats`). Money opens with a period dropdown (last month, 6 months, year, 2 or 5 years, or the owner's own dates, `cFrom`/`cTo`) and is kept simple (the owner found the analysis version too complicated): the wallet card (spent, km driven, litres, fill-ups), then "How your car is doing" with just two numbers, km per litre and what each km costs, each with one plain change ("5% better than before", "8% cheaper than before") and one summary sentence (`saySum`), then "Month by month" with one chart (spend as bars, km per litre as a smooth black line on its own axis; the owner asked for a single chart, not two) ; price per litre, how it is worked out and a table sit behind "How this is worked out". Only the last five fill-ups are listed; See all opens the rest (`allFills`). "Since a service" (`perfSince`, `perfCard`) lists only work that can change fuel use (`PERF_KINDS`, plus catalogue parts in `PERF_CAT` such as fuel filters, injectors, throttle body, ignition coils, tyre pressure; never wiper blades and the like), opens on the most recent one as "Since the last service", and compares km/L over up to 5 fill-ups before a service with up to 6 whose whole stretch of driving came after it (at least 2 on each side), and always says traffic and driving also play a part; a clear drop suggests mentioning it to the mechanic, never a diagnosis. Home offers this itself ("How is your car doing since its engine tune-up?", `perfPrompt`) for a recent tune-up, spark plugs, air filter, oil change, tyres or alignment, once per garage visit (`v.perfSeen`). "Forgot a fill-up?" (`fuelNudge`) shows on Home and Money when the days or km since the last fill-up are well over the owner's usual gap; "Not yet" hides it for 4 days (`v.fuelSnooze`). The manifest has a "Log a fill-up" shortcut (long-press the icon, `?add=fuel`). Reminding owners when they are at a petrol station needs location and background notifications, so it waits for the Play Store app (step 7).
- Estimated service dates use the owner's average km per day from the last year of records. Always label them "estimated".
- Comparisons with an earlier period only show when the records cover all of that period.
- **Phase 2 (later):** selective sharing, verification levels, garage-confirmed entries, maintenance score, buyer tools.
- Deadlines cascade: emission test, then insurance, then revenue licence. Electric and hybrid vehicles are exempt from the emission test (the DMT page says so; some 2026 guides disagree for hybrids, so hybrid owners are told to check before renewal). Rules checked against Sri Lankan sources in `research/sri-lanka-rules-check/`; keep advice on papers in line with it and never give an emission certificate validity period or penalty figures until confirmed.
- Intervals in `INTERVALS` are defaults only. The owner's manual comes first. Never state invented specs as fact.
- The Ask feature suggests questions and never diagnoses. Brakes and tyres always push toward a professional check.
- **Dashboard warning lights guide (built):** `LIGHTS` in `app.js`, wording in `strings.js` (`light_<code>_*`). 45 lights, covering cars, vans, motorbikes, three-wheelers, diesel, hybrid and electric vehicles. A light can carry `types` or `fuels`; the guide shows each owner the ones for their vehicle and shows the rest when they filter or search. `glow` gives the colour the symbol really shows (most information lights are green). Owners can record a light from its page; records live in `db.alerts`, show on Home until marked fixed (a red one takes the banner), feed the mechanic questions and the Ask context. a visual library of common dashboard lights and alerts. For each: its icon, what it usually means in plain words, how urgent it is (red: stop safely; amber: get it checked soon; green or blue: information only), what to do now, and what to ask the mechanic. It explains and suggests; it never diagnoses. Red lights such as oil pressure, engine temperature, brakes and charging always say to stop safely and get professional help. Always add "check your owner's manual", because symbols differ between makes.
- **AI review of the owner's history (planned):** the Ask assistant reads the owner's records (services, fuel, deadlines, warning lights they have looked up) and proactively recommends what to check and which questions to ask the mechanic, each one showing the record it is based on. Due dates still come from the rules engine, not the AI.

## Phase 2 groundwork already in the data
Every record has `id`, `vid` (vehicle id, separate from any owner), `createdAt`, `source` ("owner" for now; later "receipt" and "garage"), and an optional photo. Keep it that way. Never silently overwrite history: add `updatedAt` and, when sharing arrives, an audit trail. Docs, fuel and service records carry `visibility: 'private'`. Renewing a document keeps the old expiry in `history`.

## Design rules
The app is for owners who are not vehicle experts and want to know they are doing everything right. Plain words, one clear next step, details only when asked.
- Fintech look and feel (like a modern money app): soft mint-grey page with white floating cards, round quick-action buttons, and Recent activity listed like transactions with amounts on the right. Kept short so it needs little scrolling. Tabs: Home, Money, My car, Ask. Car health is not a tab because it is the first card on Home (Open Car health), and it is in the side menu. The quick buttons on Home sit under the heading "Add a record": Fill-up, Service, Document, Light, Odometer. The Health page shows parts owners often track as chips, a search that ranks the likely parts first, and a breakdown by group instead of one long list. Home, top to bottom: a red dashboard-light banner only when one is recorded, a compact Car health card (ring plus at most four parts needing attention, then Open Car health), recorded lights, Coming up as a short list of tests, insurance, licences, warranties and the owner's reminders, quick actions, and a Need help? card (A light came on, Ask about my car, What to ask my mechanic). The Health tab has the full tracker grouped by status, with search and browse. My car has papers, Your records, links, recent activity and settings. The dark green-black wallet card with a soft mint glow sits only on the Money screen. Earlier versions said "white background"; the owner later chose this finish.
- Colours follow the MotorPal logo (`marketing/logo/final/`): black #050505, deep mint #00A86B, white. `--green` is a deep mint (#007A51, readable with white text) for the brand and main actions; `--yellow` now holds the bright mint highlight (#00C47F, with black text) for the add button, dates and the car card. The token names were kept so the CSS did not need rewriting. The owner moved from green and goldfish yellow to this in November 2026. Red means needs attention now, amber means coming up soon, green means on track, always with a word next to the colour.
- A side menu (the button at the top left) lists every area, quick adds and settings. Four tabs: Home (a big banner for the single most urgent item with its own icon: a dark card; "needs attention now" is a plain dark card with no red at all, marked only by a larger honey caution triangle that blinks; "coming up soon" and "on track" show a muted honey or mint dot and hairline outline, because the owner wants the warning to blend with the black and mint, swipeable cards for what's coming up, at-a-glance tiles), My car (papers, maintenance, records, settings), Money (fuel and spending), Ask (a chat screen with an assistant avatar, bubbles, quick-question chips and a send bar; the mechanic questions are a chip). One yellow + button adds anything.
- Show, don't write: every item has its own icon (`KIND_ICON` in `app.js`), and screens lead with icons and numbers rather than sentences.
- Lists show only a name and a status. Everything else (mileage, cost, garage, oil grade) lives in the detail sheet that opens on tap. Every item's detail explains what it is and what to do in plain language (`about_*` and `todo_*` in `strings.js`).
- Plus Jakarta Sans for everything (system fonts are the offline fallback). White cards with a soft lift instead of borders; gradients only on the wallet cards. Sentence case, no all-caps labels, no emoji. Tap targets of 48px or more. Calm light finish (the "Calm light finish" block at the end of `styles.css`): a soft mint-grey page (#EEF3F0), white cards and near-black text, with the logo's black and mint kept in the fuel card, buttons and highlights. The owner tried an always-dark version in November 2026 and found it too stark for an assistant app. There is no dark mode. Background (November 2026): the owner asked to try a darker page in the brand colours, so the default is now Deep green (`:root[data-bg="deep"]`, the last block of `styles.css`): a deep green-black page (#06150F, darkened at the owner's request) with a soft mint glow. The owner found white cards too bright on it, so cards are a calm, muted sage green (#23342E, inner panels #2D433B, chosen for being easy on the eyes) with light text and mint actions (bright mint buttons with black text); the logo's black tiles stay. Contrast checked in the block's comment. Glow (November 2026, now the default): the owner shared a dark "tech" website (near-black page, dark panels with thin glowing outlines, icons in outlined circles, a glowing chart line with a fade below, stats split by thin lines) and asked for that look in our colours. It is the last block of `styles.css` (`:root[data-bg="glow"]`, building on the deep green rules, which now apply to both): near-black green page (#030B08) with a faint mint grid, panels #08150F outlined in mint at low strength, mint glows instead of fills; on Money the wallet card has a grid and glow, its three stats (km driven, litres, fill-ups) sit in outlined icon circles split by lines, and the chart line glows with a mint fade under it. Gradients and glows are allowed in this finish. Deep green (sage cards) and Light remain as choices. Light is the calm finish above. Owners switch under My car > Background (Glow, Deep green, Light) (`bgPicker`, stored per phone in `carbook.bg`). Colours typed straight into new CSS (not tokens) need a deep-green override in that block. The logo's black and mint appear as accents (the "Logo accents" block in `styles.css`): the Car health card on Home and the vehicle card in My car are black tiles with mint, the bottom tab bar is black with the selected tab in mint, and the quick buttons, the + button and the assistant avatar are black circles with mint icons. Must work offline and on small phones. Keep Sinhala and Tamil in mind: no fixed-width text, leave room for longer words.

## Known limits
- Data is stored only on this device. Clearing browser data erases it. Export regularly until cloud backup exists.
- No automatic photo reading yet. Fields are typed by hand.
- Fuel economy assumes the tank is filled each time.
- The Ask chat gives real AI answers only inside the claude.ai preview, which lets a page ask Claude. Everywhere else it answers common questions from the records with simple rules (`localAnswer` in `app.js`). Real AI everywhere needs the small CarBook server from step 3; keep `AI_RULES` as its instructions.
- Status colours were checked for readable contrast on the dark page (red and amber use lighter shades for text, `--red-text` and `--amber-text`). Re-check them if you change them.
